# Does regime detection make simple strategies more robust?

**RegimeLab research report — June 2026 (v2: adds probability scaling, the
multi-asset extension, and figures).**
All results are out-of-sample, net of transaction costs, and reproducible via
the commands in the appendix. Figures are generated by the notebooks in
`notebooks/` from experiment outputs only.

## Summary

On the S&P 500 ETF (SPY, 2005–2026, walk-forward validation, 5 bps costs),
gating buy-and-hold with either a volatility filter or a 2-state Gaussian HMM
raised the Sharpe ratio from 0.64 to ≈0.81 and cut the maximum drawdown from
−55% to −34%/−23%. Three qualifications keep this honest: (i) paired
block-bootstrap intervals show **no Sharpe difference is statistically
distinguishable from zero at 95%** over 21 years; (ii) the Sharpe edge is
concentrated in crisis eras, while the **drawdown reduction is consistent
across all eras** — regime gating is crash insurance, not alpha; (iii) on a
diversified SPY/TLT/GLD portfolio, gating adds **nothing** (vol gate) or
**hurts** (HMM): diversification already does the risk-control job. Continuous
scaling by filtered probabilities performs like the hard HMM gate on a single
asset but is clearly better than it in the multi-asset setting, where hard
thresholding amplifies state flicker on bonds and gold.

## 1. Research question

Can market regime detection improve the *robustness* — not the headline
performance — of simple strategies, out-of-sample and after costs? Does a
statistical regime model earn its complexity over a transparent heuristic? And
does the answer survive diversification?

## 2. Data and protocol

- **Data.** Adjusted closes from yfinance, cached immutably with download
  metadata. Single-asset: SPY 2000–2026. Multi-asset: SPY/TLT/GLD from
  2004-12 (GLD inception).
- **Validation.** Expanding walk-forward: 1,260-day initial train, refit every
  252 days (SPY: 22 refits, OOS 2005-01→2026-06, 5,388 days; multi-asset: 17
  refits, OOS 2009-12→2026-06). Models are fitted per asset, on past data
  only; only out-of-sample regime labels/probabilities are used.
- **Point-in-time safety.** Execution lag centralized in the engine (signal at
  close *t* earns *t+1*); HMM inference uses *filtered* probabilities from a
  manual forward recursion (smoothed inference conditions on the future);
  features, labels, and probabilities pass truncation causality tests.
- **Costs.** Proportional, 5 bps headline, swept over {0, 5, 20}.

## 3. Models and strategies

| Name | Description |
|---|---|
| `buy_and_hold` | Fully invested (equal-weight across assets) |
| `trend_200d` | Long when price > 200-day MA, else cash (per asset) |
| `vol20q80_*` | Flat when 20d realized vol > 80% quantile of training vol |
| `hmm2_*` | Flat when the 2-state HMM's filtered state is high-variance |
| `hmm2p_bh` | **Continuous**: exposure = filtered P(calm), per asset |
| `hmm3_bh` | Flat only in the most turbulent of 3 HMM states |

### 4.1 What the HMM actually detects

![SPY with HMM regimes and filtered probability](figures/spy_hmm2_regimes.png)

Turbulent labels (21% of OOS days) concentrate on 2008–09, 2011, 2015–16,
2018, March 2020, and 2022, and agree with the vol filter on most dates. The
probability panel shows the cost of hard thresholding: near regime boundaries
the filtered probability oscillates around 0.5, and every crossing is a round
trip. This flicker is why the hard HMM gate turns over 9.4×/yr vs. 3.4× for
the heuristic.

### 4.2 Probability scaling vs. hard gating

The continuous variant (`hmm2p_bh`, exposure = P(calm)) was hypothesized to
trade *less* than the hard gate. **The data falsified this**: turnover rose to
11.2×/yr, because the filtered probability moves a little almost every day,
and many small rebalances replace fewer large ones. On one asset, soft and
hard gating are statistically indistinguishable (ΔSharpe 0.01,
P(≤0) = 0.42). The multi-asset setting reverses this — see §6.

### 4.3 Inference, eras, and parameters

![Bootstrap Sharpe differences](figures/spy_bootstrap.png)

Paired circular block bootstrap (21-day blocks, 2,000 draws): **every 95%
interval on a Sharpe difference includes zero**, including
vol-gate-vs-buy-and-hold (+0.18, CI [−0.12, +0.50]). The bar for claiming
Sharpe improvement from regime timing is simply higher than 21 years of daily
data can clear.

![Sharpe by sub-period](figures/spy_subperiods.png)

The Sharpe edge lives in 2005–09 and (for the vol gate) 2020+; in the calm
2010s gating neither helped nor hurt. The drawdown reduction, by contrast,
appears in **every** era — the crash-insurance interpretation.

![Parameter sensitivity](figures/spy_sensitivity.png)

The vol-gate grid is a plateau (all cells beat buy-and-hold's 0.64), so the
headline configuration is not a tuned artifact.

## 5. Multi-asset results (SPY/TLT/GLD, equal-weight, 2010–2026, 5 bps)

![Multi-asset wealth](figures/multiasset_wealth.png)

| Strategy | Sharpe | Ann. return | Max DD | Turnover/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| buy_and_hold (EW) | 0.96 | 8.9% | −23% | 0.06× |
| vol20q80_bh | 0.98 | 7.3% | −13% | 5.0× |
| hmm2p_bh (soft) | 0.88 | 6.0% | −13% | 11.3× |
| hmm2_bh (hard) | 0.80 | 5.7% | −16% | 9.5× |
| trend_200d | 0.76 | 5.4% | −15% | 8.4× |

![Multi-asset bootstrap](figures/multiasset_bootstrap.png)

The headline result is **negative and informative**: the diversified
equal-weight portfolio already achieves Sharpe 0.96 with a −23% max drawdown,
and per-asset regime gating does not improve risk-adjusted returns (vol gate
ΔSharpe +0.02, P(≤0) = 0.45). The HMM gate actively hurts (−0.16,
P(≤0) = 0.87) — it trades bond and gold volatility states that are not
reliably associated with negative returns the way equity vol is. Gating still
halves the max drawdown (−23% → −13%), at a return cost.

Two secondary findings: soft probability scaling now clearly beats hard
gating (hard − soft ΔSharpe −0.09, P(hard ≥ soft) ≈ 0.03 — the strongest
bootstrap signal in the study), and the volatility heuristic beats the HMM
gate (+0.18, P(≤0) = 0.04).

## 6. Conclusions

1. **Regime gating is crash insurance.** It reliably cuts deep drawdowns
   (−55% → −23/34% on SPY, −23% → −13% multi-asset, in every sub-period) while
   giving up some return; it is not a Sharpe machine, and Sharpe claims do not
   survive bootstrap scrutiny at this sample size.
2. **Complexity did not pay.** The HMM never beat the volatility heuristic
   where both worked (SPY: ΔSharpe +0.01), and clearly lost where regimes are
   less vol-driven (multi-asset: −0.18 with P = 0.96). The transparent rule is
   the better default.
3. **Diversification first.** The cheapest "regime model" in the study was
   holding three lowly-correlated assets: it matched the best gated SPY
   Sharpe with near-zero turnover. Regime detection is a substitute for
   diversification more than a complement to it.
4. **If using a probabilistic model, scale — don't threshold.** Hard gates
   amplify filtered-state flicker; probability-weighted exposure dominated the
   hard gate in the multi-asset test and tied on SPY.

## 7. Limitations

- Long-only gating to cash with zero interest on cash; a realistic cash leg
  would flatter the gated strategies somewhat.
- One block length (21d) for the bootstrap; one walk-forward schedule
  (yearly, expanding). Sensitivity to these was not explored.
- Multi-asset gating is per-asset and ignores cross-asset information; a
  joint regime model (multivariate HMM) is untested here.
- yfinance data; ETF history limits the multi-asset sample to one and a half
  market cycles.

## 8. Next improvements

1. Cross-asset regime modeling: one multivariate HMM on the joint return
   vector vs. the per-asset models used here.
2. A cash-rate leg (e.g. T-bill returns when flat), which affects gated
   strategies' absolute returns materially over 2005–2007 and 2023–2026.
3. Turnover-aware scaling: trade only when the filtered probability moves by
   more than a band (hysteresis), targeting the flicker problem directly.
4. Bootstrap block-length and walk-forward-schedule sensitivity.
